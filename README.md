TazaTender

Every year, trillions of tenge pass through Kazakhstan's public procurement system—money allocated for roads, healthcare, schools, and municipal infrastructure. Formally, all of this data is public: the procurement portal provides an official open API. However, data publicity is not synonymous with accessibility. Raw data is scattered across dozens of fields and relationships, lacking aggregation, history, and any unified way to ask, "is this purchase normal compared to similar ones?" As a result, even an investigative journalist or activist has to manually cross-reference figures just to determine whether a case warrants a deeper look.

TazaTender.kz directly addresses this gap. The project daily ingests fresh data via the official API—announcements, lots, contracts, the registry of participants, and the registry of blacklisted suppliers—and consolidates it into a structured, relational database. On top of this database, the platform calculates a set of objective, reproducible metrics: the proportion of procurements conducted through "single-source" agreements rather than competitive bidding; the rate of lots receiving only a single bid; and the final price deviation from the median price of comparable procurements in the same region and category. Each metric serves not as an accusation, but as a signal: it indicates that a specific tender or buyer is statistically atypical relative to the broader market, deserving closer scrutiny from those equipped and authorized to draw conclusions—journalists, auditors, and citizens alike.

All of this is accessible through a clean web interface: search functionality by organization or BIN (Business Identification Number), profile cards containing full procurement histories alongside calculated metrics, a weekly dashboard highlighting the most prominent deviations, and a dedicated methodology page explaining in clear language what is being calculated, how it works, and what these figures do not imply. In addition to the web platform, the project exposes its data through an open API, enabling third-party researchers and software developers to build upon the data independently.

A cornerstone of the project is its ethical architecture. The system never frames its findings using terms like "corruption" or "violation": it presents statistical facts and leaves interpretation to professionals who verify facts. Every organization retains the ability to submit clarifications or rebuttals. The project's codebase is open-source under the MIT license, and the aggregated datasets are available under open-data terms requiring attribution—positioning the project as shared public infrastructure rather than a closed commercial product.

The value of TazaTender.kz is measured not by web traffic, but by the quality of connections it fosters: how many investigative reports originated from anomalies discovered on the platform, how many independent developers and researchers utilize the open API, and how many procurement entities and categories are covered under continuous monitoring. This represents a gradual, cumulative growth model—the very process that transforms a project into genuine civic infrastructure that delivers lasting public value over time.
